How This Analysis Works

Questions asked, data used, and the method behind every number in report.html and report_acmg_vs_clinvar.html

The two questions

  1. Penetrance: among people who carry a likely-pathogenic variant in a cancer-predisposition gene, what fraction actually develop the cancer that gene is known to cause, and at what age?
  2. Concordance: does relying on ClinVar to confirm a variant's pathogenicity work equally well for every ancestry group, or does it systematically miss carriers in some groups more than others?

Both questions are asked of the same AoU carrier cohort, so the same denominator-building logic underlies both.

Building the canonical carrier denominator

Every gene-level number in the report (penetrance, ancestry breakdown, evidence-gap %) is computed from one canonical carrier set per gene, built in this order:

1
Start from raw variant rows for the gene — every (person, variant) row, including multiple variants per person.
2
Collapse to unique person × gene. A person counts as a carrier once per gene, and as ACMG-PLP/ClinVar-PLP for that gene if any of their variants in it carries that flag.
3
Restrict to ACMG-PLP carriers. We use ACMG (rule-based, applied uniformly regardless of ancestry) rather than ClinVar-PLP as the carrier definition — restricting to ClinVar-PLP would bake the ancestry-based confirmation gap (Finding 1) directly into the penetrance denominator.
4
Join to the cohort table to get phenotype/age/sex, using a person-level cohort table built by first collapsing the cohort's multiple per-person rows into (a) one demographic row and (b) a set of every disease Group that person has ever been recorded under. Carriers with no matching cohort record are dropped here and reported explicitly (they have no phenotype data, so they cannot contribute to a penetrance calculation).
5
Restrict to the correct sex for genes whose mapped cancer type(s) are sex-specific (e.g. breast/ovarian/endometrial genes are restricted to female carriers, both numerator and denominator).

Excluding genotyping artifacts

Some genes sit in or near a pseudogene / highly-homologous paralog region, where short-read mapping can generate spurious, ancestry-skewed carrier calls that are a sequencing artifact, not biology. We flag a gene if:

  • it is PMS2 (a known PMS2/PMS2CL pseudogene mismapping problem), or
  • it is on a small watch-list of other pseudogene-adjacent genes (CHEK2, SMAD4) and one non-European ancestry has more than 2× the European-ancestry carrier count — a pattern with no plausible biological explanation.

PMS2Known PMS2/PMS2CL pseudogene mismapping artifact

Flagged genes are excluded from the main penetrance results and figures, and listed separately in excluded_genes.tsv and the report's "Excluded" table — never silently dropped.

Disease-specific penetrance, not "any cancer"

For each gene we use an explicit gene → cancer-type mapping (e.g. BRCA1 → Breast, Ovary) rather than counting any recorded cancer as "affected." A BRCA1 carrier with lung cancer is not counted as a BRCA1 penetrance event. Each mapped cancer type is reported and plotted as its own line — BRCA1:Breast and BRCA1:Ovary are never merged into one number. People recorded only under "Family history" (no personal diagnosis) are explicitly excluded from the numerator by an assertion in the code.

p̂gene,cancer,age  =  N(canonical carriers with matched cancer)N(all canonical carriers in that age band)

Every carrier rate is compared to a population background rate: the same disease's prevalence in the whole cohort (not just carriers of that gene), computed in the same age bands and with the same sex restriction, so the comparison is apples-to-apples.

r̂bg, disease, age  =  N(whole cohort with that exact disease)N(whole cohort in that age band)

A gene-disease-age cell is marked "significantly elevated" only when:

CIlower(carrier rate)  >  CIupper(background rate)

A conservative non-overlapping-interval test. Because this cohort is a case/control/family-history study design rather than a random population sample, this background reflects the cohort's own case-mix, not a true general-population incidence rate.

All proportions above use a 95% Wilson score interval, not the normal approximation (several cells here have small N or proportions near 0/1, where the normal approximation undercoverages):

CI  =  p̂ + z2/2n  ±  z√(p̂(1−p̂)/n + z2/4n2) 1 + z2/n

z = 1.96 (95% level), n = the matching denominator above. Computed via statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportion_confint(method="wilson").

Concordance footnotes

The headline concordance-by-ancestry numbers are ACMG-PLP carriers who are not also ClinVar-PLP, by ancestry. Two footnote checks guard against over-reading a single high number:

  • Singleton fraction: what share of discordant variant instances are carried by only one person in the dataset (a rarity proxy)? A high fraction would suggest the gap is about rare, hard-to -classify variants rather than a systemic curation gap.
  • Recurrent-variant share: what fraction of an ancestry's discordant instances trace back to its single most common variant? A high share means one variant is driving the headline number, and it should be read as "this one variant's status," not broad under-curation.

Applying this check surfaced a real finding: EAS discordance (91%) is 84% attributable to a single recurrent MSH6 variant, with a low singleton fraction — reported as a specific-variant caveat in the main report rather than removed, per the review that requested this check.

Limitations of the method itself

  • The "ACMG-PLP" restriction still depends on the upstream ACMG classification pipeline being correct; we did not re-derive ACMG criteria here.
  • Population background rates are computed on this same ascertained cohort (a healthcare-utilizing volunteer population), not a true general-population rate.
  • Age bands are coarse (5 bins); a finer-grained or continuous age model would use more of the data but was out of scope for this pass.
  • The pseudogene-artifact rule is a heuristic (PMS2 always flagged; a 2× EUR-ratio rule for two other watch-list genes) and does not exhaustively check every gene for mapping problems.
